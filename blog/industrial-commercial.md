Industrial and commercial buildings are usually driven by what happens inside
them. A production hall is shaped by crane runways and clear spans, a data centre
by floor loading and plant, an office by flexibility and speed of construction.
The structure follows from the operation rather than the other way round.

## Where the load actually comes from

In housing, the imposed loads are predictable and modest. In industrial and
commercial work they are neither. Equipment loads, crane loads, and
concentrated plant loads frequently exceed everything else acting on the frame.

Data centres are the clearest example. Floor loading from server racks, standby
generation, cooling plant and uninterruptible power supplies can be several times
what an office of the same footprint would ever see. Deflection limits are also
tighter than the structural minimum, because the equipment and its containment
will not tolerate movement. Getting these numbers agreed early matters more than
almost any other decision, since a change to rack density late in design can
force the frame to be reworked from the foundations up.

Production halls bring a different problem. Crane loading is dynamic, repeated,
and applied through the runway into the columns as horizontal surge as well as
vertical load. Fatigue becomes a real consideration in a way it rarely is in
building work.

## Construction method as a design input

On commercial projects, programme is usually worth more to the client than
material efficiency. That pushes work toward prefabricated concrete, where
elements are cast off site and assembled quickly, and toward composite floor
systems that reduce propping and follow-on trades.

Prefabrication changes the engineering rather than simplifying it. Connections
carry the design, transport and lifting govern element sizes, and tolerances have
to be resolved on paper because there is little scope to adjust on site. The
analysis has to cover the temporary condition during erection as well as the
finished structure, and the temporary case is often the more demanding one.

## Specialist facilities

Some buildings are governed by requirements that sit outside normal building
design entirely. We have carried out structural planning for an experimental
facility including a bunker for explosive testing, where the containment
structure and the surrounding accommodation had to be designed to different
principles on the same site.

Work of this kind is governed by the specified threat and the performance the
client requires, and it needs to be established before the structural concept is
fixed rather than checked afterwards.

## Standards and tools

Work is carried out to the Eurocodes as adopted in Germany, DIN EN 1990 through
1998. Analysis uses SOFiSTiK, Dlubal, RFEM and SCIA, with GGU for foundation and
geotechnical checks. Detailing is produced in Nemetschek, AutoCAD and Tekla.
